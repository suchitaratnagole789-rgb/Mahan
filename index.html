THE ENERGY QUESTION

Our energy choices
shape our world.

Growing cities need reliable energy for homes, transport, businesses and public services. But the way we produce that energy affects air quality, climate, ecosystems and household costs.

Solar power offers one path toward a cleaner energy mix. Understanding both its potential and its limitations helps communities make informed decisions.

01 — RENEWABLE ENERGY

Meet the power
of the sun.

Solar photovoltaic (PV) panels convert sunlight directly into electricity. An inverter converts the electricity into a form used by buildings and the grid. Batteries can store some of the energy for later use.

  • Renewable source replenished by sunlight
  • No direct air pollution while generating electricity
  • Can be installed on rooftops and unused land
  • Output varies with weather, daylight and location

02 — CONVENTIONAL ENERGY

Powering today.
Considering the cost.

Coal, oil and natural gas have powered industry and electricity systems for decades. They can provide energy when needed, but burning fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases and air pollutants.

  • Established infrastructure in many regions
  • Fuel can often be stored and dispatched
  • Combustion releases carbon dioxide and other pollutants
  • Fuel extraction and transport can affect land and water
